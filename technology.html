Threshold · drift · physics · the filter

Why physics beats a threshold.

Four ideas, each in one technical line and one plain sentence.

Concept
Technically
In plain words
Threshold
Fires only when a variable crosses a fixed limit.
A tripwire — it only shouts after you've already hit it.
Drift detection
Tracks continuous deviation from expected physical behaviour.
It notices the slow slide while the numbers still look fine.
Real-time physics
Cross-checks readings against the governing physical laws of the asset.
It knows what's possible, not just what's high.
The missing filter
SCADA displays whatever it receives — noise, frozen sensors, out-of-order or injected data.
SCADA believes its sensors. PDIS-16 checks whether they're telling the truth.
You can fake a sensor reading. You can't fake physics.

Because every value is validated against physical law, a reading manipulated to look normal but inconsistent with the system's physics is rejected — regardless of whether its network login was valid. The physics is the filter attackers can't talk their way past.

Where it fits

PLC controls. SCADA displays. PDIS-16 validates the physics.

Not a replacement for anything you run — a physics layer that integrates into both.

CONTROL LAYER

PLC

Runs high-speed control loops — open the valve, stop the motor. Excellent at logic; blind to multi-variable physics.

PHYSICS LAYER

PDIS-16

Validates cross-sensor causality and physical law in real time, and surfaces drift before failure. Integrates into your existing SCADA and PLC.

DISPLAY LAYER

SCADA

Renders sensor numbers and sets static red-line alarms for operators. Sees only what it's shown — after a limit breaks.
